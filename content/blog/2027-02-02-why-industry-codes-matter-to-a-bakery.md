A bakery makes bread, rolls, pies and pastries. On paper, it is also a number. Most bakery owners and general managers come across that number sooner or later, on a form, a bid, a loan application or an industry report. It is called an industry code, and it is worth knowing what yours is and what it means.

This article explains the code system used in North America, which codes apply to bakeries, and why getting the code right saves trouble.

## What an industry code is

The system is called NAICS, the North American Industry Classification System. It gives every kind of business a code of up to six digits. Each code stands for a type of activity, and related codes are grouped together.

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) describes the basic idea in one sentence: "NAICS codes classify businesses based on the particular product or service they supply." A business generally has a primary code, and it may have more than one if it sells several products or services.

## The codes that apply to bakeries

Two codes cover most bakeries, and they sit side by side. IBISWorld shows the path for commercial bakeries: 31 is Manufacturing, 311 is Food Manufacturing, 3118 is Bakeries and Tortilla Manufacturing, 31181 is Bread and Bakery Product Manufacturing, and 311812 is Commercial Bakeries. Retail bakeries, 311811, sit under the same group, 31181.

The first two digits give the broad sector, and each added digit narrows it. A six-digit code tells a reader quite a lot in very little space.

:::note Worth knowing
Per IBISWorld, NAICS 311812 and 311811 both fall under 31181, Bread and Bakery Product Manufacturing.
:::

## What commercial bakeries cover

IBISWorld describes NAICS 311812 as covering establishments primarily engaged in manufacturing fresh and frozen bread, bread-type rolls and other fresh bakery products. Cookies and crackers are excluded. The examples it lists include croissants, bagels, fresh pies, doughnuts that are not frozen, soft pretzels, matzo, communion wafers, croutons and bread crumbs, all made in commercial bakeries.

So a plant that bakes bread and rolls for stores and restaurants, or one that bakes bagels at scale, fits here. The word "commercial" points to manufacturing rather than selling over a counter.

## What retail bakeries cover

IBISWorld says NAICS 311811 is for retailing bread and other bakery products that are not for immediate consumption and that are made on the premises from flour rather than prepared dough. This is the code for a bakery that makes its products on site and sells them directly.

If your shop bakes from scratch where customers buy, 311811 is the closer match. If your main business is producing baked goods to be sold elsewhere, 311812 is the closer match. Some businesses do both, which is one reason a business may carry more than one code.

## Where other products go

The line between codes is drawn by what a business mainly does. IBISWorld lists several nearby codes to show where products outside 311812 belong:

1. Frozen bakery products other than bread go to 311813, Frozen Cakes, Pies, and Other Pastries Manufacturing.
2. Cookies and crackers go to 311821, Cookie and Cracker Manufacturing.
3. Pretzels other than soft pretzels go to 311919, Other Snack Food Manufacturing.
4. Retailing bakery products made elsewhere and not for immediate consumption goes to 445291, Baked Goods Stores.
5. Preparing and selling bakery products for immediate consumption goes to 722515, Snack and Nonalcoholic Beverage Bars.

If your product list crosses these lines, read the full description for each code before you decide.

## Where the code shows up

The code is not only a label. It is used in several places.

The SBA assigns a size standard to each NAICS code. That means the threshold that defines a small business depends on the code attached to your business. The SBA also says contracting officers assign a NAICS code to each contract, and that the designation can be appealed to the SBA's Office of Hearings and Appeals within 10 days of the publication of the solicitation.

Industry research uses the codes as well. IBISWorld organizes its industry data by NAICS code, and its page for the parent group 31181 reports 2026 revenue of $59.1 billion and growth of 2.91 percent. Those figures are IBISWorld's, and they cover the whole group rather than a single bakery.

## How to check your own code

Start with the description, not the number. Read the IBISWorld pages for 311812 and 311811 and compare them with what you actually make and where you sell it. Then check the code against the paperwork you already use. If a form lists a code that does not match what you do, ask the agency or lender how to correct it, since the size standard and the way you are grouped in industry reports both follow from the code.

## The short version

NAICS sorts businesses by what they supply. Commercial bakeries fall under 311812 and retail bakeries under 311811, and both sit within 31181. The code you carry affects small business size standards and how you appear in industry data, so it is worth confirming that yours matches your operation.
